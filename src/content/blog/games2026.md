I constantly find myself having a harder time enjoying most popular videogames and not for a lack of trying, *Vampire Survivors*, *Clair Obscure 33*, *Balatro*, *Peak*, I tried but they are just not for me at all, same with most AAA productions. Thankfully **diversity is a strenght in the videogame industry**, so here are games the have caught my attention and why...

[background[0]]

## .45 PARABELLUM BLOODHOUND - Cyberpunk Active Time Action
 
Based on the trailer alone there is a lot that I appreciate from the game:
<ol>
  <li>First the *PSX graphics* with a gorgeous color palette and special effects (see the rain drop texture on the camera).</li>
  <li>The combat system reminds me of Vagrant Story.</li>
  <li>The music transmits a darkness and fast-pace action, urgency, 2000's vibes.</li>
</ol>

Most of all my attention was drawn by the cinematic flare of the game; In just a few scenes I can tell the author or whoever is in charge of the cinematics/cutscenes has a good understanding and employment on cinematic language, there is excellent blocking, camera angles, use of lighting and perspective, far, far better than most AAA's I've played lately.

### Sword Hero 

[background[2]]

I must admit *a strong dislike for the term Dark Fantasy* and everything related to it, as to me its just an excuse for adults to feel better about consuming fiction, as if darkness, violence, gore, hopelessness, desaturated colors and slow forgettable music are a justification for them to not feel shame about their interests. 

And somehow even though *Forestware's *Sword Hero screams of Dark Fantasy*, I can't help but be attracted to it. The game's trailer and it's description make it clear this is an old-school rpg. This solo dev project has been in development for some 7 years and it truly looks like an author's unique and untouched work in the medium, it's scale full of many complex systems it's a commendable feat for a solo dev, even before early access the game already counts with:

<ol>
  <li>Lock-picking mini-game.</li>
  <li>Crime and punishment system.</li>
  <li>Parkour | Level traversal.</li>
  <li>Action limb-based combat.</li>
  <li>Smart AI | Enviromental interaction</li>
  <li>World Factions.</li>
  <li>Towns and cities filled with NPCs(routines)</li>
  <li>Equipment | Inventort |Trading system</li>
</ol>

This will surely be a monumental role playing game that I hope gets released eventually. The developer uploads blogs on development, plans and details. I say let him cook!

[scene[6], "Minimal, stylized HUD"]
[scene[7], "Class system"]
[scene[8], "Colorful scenaries for a dark fantasy game are a welcome change"]

## Deep Dish Dungeon

[background[6]]

As I mentioned previously I don't like most popular games and yes that includes **Minecraft** I know, I know, that could be considered a sin coming from a game developer, but I just can't click with them, and yet here this 4 player co-op game caught my attention. From the trailer I can tell there is a good level of polish I often find lacking in most online co-op games, the setting looks interesting, food/cooking is a main mechanic, and it scratches a fantasy I always had of going through the mines of Moria, surrounded by dangers, darkness, hunger, with only your fellow companions as support, it's coming out soon, just as I'm unemployed (yay I guess?).

## G String

[background[8]]

Another cyberpunk entry, I really don't know how to go about this one, there is so much I like, the steam capsule alone caught my eye. Turns out this game took 10 years to developed ("10 yearsssss!" Matt Smith in House of the Dragon), as a solo project, this is a formidable feat, it takes a great level of dedication and most of all discipline to achieve, assets were mostly done by the developer, just that fact makes me want to try it.

By the time of this blog being written the game has come out, to some recent mixed reviews... but I'll play it anyways, it's a feat that few game devs manage to do, and just because of that I'll give it the benefit of the doubt. This is someone's vision, in the screenshots and trailer I see lot of style, a world I want to explore and gameplay to get proficient at.

## Reconfigure

[background[9]]

During the pandemic on my second year of college studying for game dev, I got to play this little game, called System Shock. It was so addictive I almost failed a math course, the level design, the puzzles, the details, the weird music (which turns out is autogenerated or something like if I recall correctly), it was my first ever immersive sim. While looking for more cyberpunk/sci-fi sims I found Reconfigure, it has an artstyle I love which is low-poly with pixel textures and a cool shading for the game's dark areas. The trailer while not very eye-catching shows true gameplay in it's stage of development, which is a level of sincerity and transparency I wish more game developers had.

[scene[28], "Love the way light looks with the dithering shader"]
